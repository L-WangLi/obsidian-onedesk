# English

A few minutes of speaking practice a day, on what you did yesterday, so there's no hunting for a topic.

![English](../screenshots/06-english.webp)

## The idea

The biggest obstacle to speaking practice is often *what do I talk about today?* OneDesk fixes the **form** by weekday and takes the **content** from the tasks you completed the day before. What to say is already in your head; only the English is missing. Topics never run out and never repeat.

## The week

| Day | Form | At least |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Part 1 · short answers, for fluency | 5 min |
| Tue | Part 2 · a 2-minute talk | 2 min |
| Wed | Part 3 · discussion, for opinions | 4 min |
| Thu | Part 1 | 5 min |
| Fri | Part 2 | 2 min |
| Sat | Long sentences · read aloud English sentences you highlighted in papers, with their translations | 5 min |
| Sun | Rest | — |

Durations are a floor, not a target. The forms follow the IELTS speaking test: Part 2 cue cards are matched to the kind of thing you did; Part 3 draws on a fixed question bank grouped by area.

## How to practise

1. **Say it through without correcting yourself.**
2. **Paste what you said into an AI and ask for corrections.**
3. **Say the corrected version again.**

- **做完了** (done) logs the day in `Drills/Sessions.md`, e.g. `- Day 3 · Part 2 · finished the setup section · 2min`.
- **＋ 记一句表达** (keep a phrase) asks for what you wanted to say in your own language and the English the AI gave you, saved as `  - 中文 :: English`.
- **今天太忙 · 降成 Part 1** (too busy — drop to Part 1) on a full day.

The page also shows this week's progress, the practice log and the phrases you kept. Material comes from yesterday's daily note, or today's when yesterday has none.
