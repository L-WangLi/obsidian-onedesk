# English

Two drills: **Patterns** practises chunks — one pattern, four variations, ten full sentences; **Speaking** is a few minutes a day on what you did yesterday.

![English](../screenshots/06-english.webp)

## Patterns

Chunks rather than single words. Each pattern comes in **four variations** (for *be going to*: a plan and what follows / not until / asking someone's plan / was going to, but) with **ten sentences long enough to carry a thought**, for example:

> 我今晚要看一下实验结果，再决定要不要换方法。
> I'm gonna check the experiment results tonight and decide whether I need to change the method.

**A four-week plan**, five patterns a week, twenty in all:

| Week | Theme | Register | Patterns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plans | Everyday American speech | be going to / gonna · I'm thinking of · be supposed to · be about to · I'd rather … than |
| 2 | Experience and change | IELTS speaking Part 1/2 | have been doing · used to · It's been ages since · The first time I · It turned out |
| 3 | Opinion and concession | IELTS Part 3 · TOEFL speaking | It depends on · The thing is · Even though · I'm not saying … but · What really matters is |
| 4 | Cause and hypothesis | TOEFL · academic discussion | If I had … would have · That's why · As long as · The more … the more · It's worth …ing |

- **Monday to Friday** bring the next unfinished pattern; a missed day postpones it rather than skipping it. **Saturday** mixes ten sentences from the week's patterns, shown in Chinese only. **Sunday** is off, though you can still practise. After all twenty, a second round starts from the one practised longest ago.
- **How**: read the Chinese and say it aloud first → type it and press Enter to save and reveal the reference → compare; the meaning matters, not the exact words. **练完了** marks the pattern done.
- ‹ › browses the other patterns.
- Practice goes into the same English log, one line per pattern with your sentences under it: `- Day 3 · 句型 · be going to / gonna · 10/10 · ✓`, `  - 中文 → English`.
- The sentences are original, written in those registers around a graduate student's research and daily life; nothing is quoted from shows or exam papers.

## Speaking


The biggest obstacle to speaking practice is often *what do I talk about today?* OneDesk fixes the **form** by weekday and takes the **content** from the tasks you completed the day before. What to say is already in your head; only the English is missing. Topics never run out and never repeat.

### The week

| Day | Form | At least |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Part 1 · short answers, for fluency | 5 min |
| Tue | Part 2 · a 2-minute talk | 2 min |
| Wed | Part 3 · discussion, for opinions | 4 min |
| Thu | Part 1 | 5 min |
| Fri | Part 2 | 2 min |
| Sat | Long sentences · read aloud English sentences you highlighted in papers, with their translations | 5 min |
| Sun | Rest | — |

Durations are a floor, not a target. The forms follow the IELTS speaking test: Part 2 cue cards are matched to the kind of thing you did; Part 3 draws on a fixed question bank grouped by area.

### How to practise

1. **Say it through without correcting yourself.**
2. **Paste what you said into an AI and ask for corrections.**
3. **Say the corrected version again.**

- **做完了** (done) logs the day in `Drills/Sessions.md`, e.g. `- Day 3 · Part 2 · finished the setup section · 2min`.
- **＋ 记一句表达** (keep a phrase) asks for what you wanted to say in your own language and the English the AI gave you, saved as `  - 中文 :: English`.
- **今天太忙 · 降成 Part 1** (too busy — drop to Part 1) on a full day.

The page also shows this week's progress, the practice log and the phrases you kept. Material comes from yesterday's daily note, or today's when yesterday has none.
